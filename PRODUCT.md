# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Calgary-area residents, commuters, visitors, community volunteers, and local organizations who want a quick picture of recent incidents near a place they care about. They may be checking before leaving home, responding to something they observed, or deciding whether a situation belongs with neighbours, a municipal service, police non-emergency, or 911.

## Product Purpose

Calgary Watch provides a free, public incident map that combines community observations with clearly attributed public-source information. Its primary job is local awareness: help people see what has recently been reported nearby and let signed-in residents share useful, time-sensitive observations. Success means more residents can find the map, understand the provenance and limits of each report, act through the correct official channel when needed, and contribute without Calgary Watch being mistaken for a police service.

## Tagline

**See it. Share it. Calgary knows.**

This is the locked brand line. It appears verbatim — same three beats, same
order — on the landing hero (as the H1), the mobile navigation panel, the site
footer, the closing call to action, the About page, and in page metadata. Do not
write variants of it. Repetition across surfaces is what gives it force.

The line encodes the positioning: *See it* is the resident who witnesses
something, *Share it* is the thirty-second report, and *Calgary knows* is the
result — the city holding a shared picture rather than five partial ones.

## Positioning

Calgary Watch is a layer, not another source. The City of Calgary 311 holds
service requests, ENMAX holds outages, the Calgary Police Service holds file
records and after-the-fact statistics, and Reddit and neighbourhood Facebook
groups hold unstructured discussion. Each is right about one piece; none of them
is about the block a resident lives on, right now, in one view. Calgary Watch is
where community observations and clearly attributed public-source items are read
together.

Naming those services is permitted, but only on the About page and in
AI-readable overviews, where there is room to state plainly that Calgary Watch is
not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by any of them, and does not
replace any of them. Never name them on the landing hero or in a page title,
where the claim would sit without its qualification and could read as official
affiliation. See Anti-references below.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, alert, civic. The voice is direct, calm, neighbourly, and precise about uncertainty. It creates useful awareness without manufacturing fear or presenting unverified observations as confirmed facts.

## Anti-references

- Police or emergency-service impersonation, including badges, scanner aesthetics, or language implying official dispatch access.
- Fear-driven crime sites that rank neighbourhoods as dangerous without current, attributable evidence.
- Anonymous social feeds that reward outrage, speculation, or identification of private individuals.
- Generic SaaS marketing that obscures the live map behind abstract claims and conversion theatre.
- Thin, duplicated location pages created only to capture search terms.

## Design Principles

- Put the map and current local context before marketing claims.
- State provenance, time, uncertainty, and limitations wherever they affect interpretation.
- Turn discovery into a useful next action: view nearby reports, use an official reporting channel, or sign in to contribute.
- Grow through specific public value and trustworthy citations, not sensationalism or keyword repetition.
- Make community participation feel neighbourly while keeping safety-critical actions unmistakable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. All primary flows must support keyboard navigation, screen readers, reduced motion, 200% zoom, and touch use. Status and severity cannot depend on colour alone. Text must remain readable in bright outdoor conditions and on small mobile screens. Emergency and non-emergency guidance must use plain language and must not assume familiarity with Calgary institutions.
